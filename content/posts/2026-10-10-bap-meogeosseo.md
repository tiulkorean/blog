In K-dramas, people ask **밥 먹었어?** (*bap meogeosseo?*, “Have you eaten?”) at the most dramatic moments — after a breakup, in a hospital hallway, at 2 a.m. outside someone’s apartment.

That’s because in Korean it’s rarely just about food. Asking whether someone has eaten is one of the most natural ways to show **you care about them**, especially when saying it directly would feel too heavy.

## Adding 은 changes the feeling

Listen closely and you’ll often hear **밥은 먹었어?** with an extra **은**. That little particle adds a sense of *at least*: “Did you *at least* eat?” — the kind of thing a worried friend, parent or love interest says.

## “Let’s eat sometime” isn’t always a plan

**언제 밥 한번 먹자** (“Let’s grab a meal sometime”) is a friendly way to end a conversation. Like *we should catch up sometime* in English, it doesn’t always mean a real date is coming!
