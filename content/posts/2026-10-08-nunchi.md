If you spend any time in Korea, you will hear the word **눈치** (*nunchi*) constantly. It starts with 눈 (*nun*, “eye”) — a nice hint at its meaning — and it describes the ability to sense what other people are feeling — or what a situation calls for — **without being told**.

English has close cousins like *reading the room* or *social awareness*, but none of them carry quite the same weight. In Korea, having good nunchi is treated as a real life skill, especially at work and in groups.

## Fast, slow, or none at all

Koreans usually describe nunchi as something that can be **quick** (빠르다) or **missing** (없다). Being called 눈치가 빠르다 is a compliment; 눈치가 없다 is a gentle (or not so gentle) criticism.

## “Watching nunchi”

눈치를 보다 — literally “to look at nunchi” — means carefully watching how others react before you act. It often describes feeling a little uneasy: waiting to see whether it’s okay to leave the office before your manager, or whether you should take the last piece of food.

## 눈치껏: figure it out yourself

When a Korean speaker says **눈치껏 해** (“do it with nunchi”), they are trusting you to work out the right thing on your own. It sounds casual, but it can be one of the most demanding instructions you’ll ever get!
